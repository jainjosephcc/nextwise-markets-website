Why trade forex CFDs?

The foreign-exchange market reflects relative changes between global currencies. Forex CFDs let eligible clients take long or short positions without taking delivery of either currency, while leverage can magnify losses.

Understanding currency pairs

Currency pairs represent the exchange rate between two currencies. The value tells you how much of the quote currency corresponds to one unit of the base currency.

Illustrative price example

If GBP/USD is quoted at 1.15, one British pound corresponds to 1.15 US dollars at that quoted price.

  • If GBP/USD moves from 1.15 to 1.16, the Pound has gained strength over the Dollar.
  • If it drops to 1.13, the Pound has weakened compared to the Dollar.

Forex FAQs

What is a forex CFD?

A forex CFD tracks the price movement of a currency pair without transferring ownership of either currency. CFDs are leveraged products and can result in substantial losses.

Which currency pairs may be available?

Availability can include major and minor currency pairs and may vary by account type, jurisdiction and current product offering. Review the latest instrument specifications in MT5 before placing an order.

When is the forex market open?

Forex generally trades across the global business week, but individual symbol sessions and holiday schedules can differ. Check the current trading session shown in MT5.

Why can forex spreads change?

Spreads can widen when liquidity is lower or volatility increases, including around market openings, economic announcements and holidays. Current pricing is displayed in the platform.

Where can I review forex contract details?

Open the relevant symbol specification in MT5 to review trading hours, contract size, margin and other current conditions. Client Services can help you locate this information.
