Why trade index CFDs?

Indices summarize the combined price movement of a defined group of shares. Index CFDs let eligible clients take long or short positions on the benchmark, while leverage can magnify losses.

Understanding index CFDs

An index CFD is a contract that tracks the price movement of a stock-market benchmark, such as a US, UK or Japanese index. It does not provide ownership of the underlying shares.

Illustrative price example

  • If an index moves from 4,500 to 4,550 points, a long CFD position gains value while a short position loses value.
  • If the index falls from 4,500 to 4,450 points, the reverse applies. Fees and execution prices also affect the result.

Index CFD FAQs

What is an index CFD?

An index CFD tracks the price movement of a market benchmark without transferring ownership of its component shares. CFDs are leveraged products and can result in substantial losses.

Which index CFDs may be available?

Availability may include CFDs that track major regional and global equity benchmarks. The current list depends on account type, jurisdiction and product offering; review it in MT5.

Do I own the shares within an index?

No. An index CFD tracks the movement of a benchmark without transferring ownership of the companies represented in that index.

When can index CFDs be traded?

Each index CFD has its own session, often influenced by the underlying market's hours. Holiday schedules and breaks can differ, so check the current symbol specification in MT5.

What can affect index CFD pricing?

Pricing can react to company earnings, economic data, interest-rate expectations, political events and changes in market liquidity. Leverage magnifies both favourable and adverse price moves.
